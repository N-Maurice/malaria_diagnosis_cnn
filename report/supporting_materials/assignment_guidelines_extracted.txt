  3.1 Custom ResNet
  A residual CNN designed by the group — not a pretrained ResNet. Must make appropriate use of
  residual/skip connections and be trained from scratch on the malaria dataset, built using Keras'
  Subclassing API (custom Layer / Model subclasses with an explicit call method). The residual-
  connection design follows He et al. (2016), Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition ; the
  subclassing pattern follows the TensorFlow guide to making new layers and models via
  subclassing .
  Target: ≥90% sensitivity and ≥90% specificity on the held-out test set (see §5.1 for how this compares
  to the transfer-learning models).

  3.2 Transfer Learning Model 1
  A pretrained architecture (VGG16, ResNet50, MobileNet, EfficientNet, DenseNet, or another justified
  choice), fine-tuned on the malaria classification task.

  3.3 Transfer Learning Model 2
  A second, different pretrained architecture from Model 1, also fine-tuned on the task.

  3.4 Additional Custom Model (4-Person Groups)
  The fourth member designs and trains an additional custom CNN — a different design from the group's
  ResNet in §3.1 (e.g. a deep plain CNN, or another custom architecture such as an Inception- or
  DenseNet-style design). It must be implemented layer-by-layer rather than instantiated from a
  framework's pretrained/library architecture — the same rule already applied to the ResNet.
  Target: ≥90% sensitivity and ≥90% specificity on the held-out test set — the same bar as the custom
  ResNet (see §5.1).

  3.5 Individual Ownership Rules
       Every member owns exactly one model outright — training, the 7+ experiments, documentation of
       the decisions made, and the corresponding report section.
       3-person groups: 1 custom ResNet + 2 transfer-learning models.
       4-person groups: the same three, plus the additional custom model from §3.4.

  3.6 Non-Participation Policy
  If a member genuinely does not want to participate, that member receives an automatic zero.
  If the team leader does not report the non-participation, it affects the whole group's grade instead —
  non-reporting is treated as the group covering for the member.
  It is the group's responsibility to deliver at least 3 models regardless of a member's non-participation.
  The remaining members must cover the gap; the model count floor does not drop.

  5. Evaluation

  5.1 Metrics
  For every experiment, report accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and ROC-AUC in a table — the per-
  experiment record that shows progression across the 7+ runs. In malaria-diagnostics literature
  (including WHO's), sensitivity = recall on the infected class and specificity = true-negative rate on the
  uninfected class; report results under both sets of names.
  Performance target. WHO has not published one universal accuracy threshold for AI diagnostic
  classifiers — its general guidance instead requires developers to pre-specify their own metrics and
  thresholds, then validate on an external dataset representative of the target population
  (WHO, Regulatory considerations on artificial intelligence for health, 2023 ). Malaria specifically
  prioritizes sensitivity as the clinically critical metric — WHO's own minimum criteria for procured rapid
  tests already require ≥75% detection at low parasite density with a false-positive rate under 10% (WHO
  Global Malaria Programme ) — because missing an infection is the costlier error.
  On the specific dataset this assignment uses (NIH/NLM's 27,558-image parasitized/uninfected cell set),
  published CNN classifiers routinely clear both figures: a 16-layer custom CNN reached 96.99%
  sensitivity and 97.75% specificity, outperforming a transfer-learning baseline on the same images
  (89.00% / 94.98%) (Rajaraman et al., NIH/LHNCBC ); a sensitivity-optimized custom CNN reached

  97.05% sensitivity (95% CI 96.23–97.70%) with an AUC of 0.9876 on a held-out split of the same
  dataset (Deep learning–assisted malaria microscopy, PMC ).
  Recommended targets (held-out test set): transfer-learning models (§3.2, §3.3) — ≥95%
  sensitivity and ≥90% specificity, grounded in the published CNN literature on this exact dataset
  above; custom, hand-implemented models (the ResNet in §3.1, and the additional model in §3.4
  for 4-person groups) — ≥90% sensitivity and ≥90% specificity, a lower bar reflecting that these are
  trained from scratch with no pretrained weights. Treat both as targets to report and discuss against, not
  hard pass/fail gates — see §5.3 for why a gated numeric threshold can work against the assignment's
  actual goal of systematic experimentation.

  5.2 Required Visualizations
  For the final version of each model (not every experiment), produce and interpret four visualizations:
    1. Training vs. validation accuracy curve
    2. Training vs. validation loss curve
    3. Confusion matrix
    4. ROC/AUC curve
  Note: ROC-AUC appears twice by design — as a single number in the §5.1 metrics table, and as a full
  curve here showing the sensitivity/specificity trade-off across all thresholds. These are not redundant.
  Visualizations must be labeled and interpreted, not just pasted in.

  5.3 Error Analysis
  Examine misclassified examples per model and discuss likely causes. Identify signs of overfitting or
  underfitting and explain what was attempted to address them.

  5.4 Explainability
  For each of the three final models, apply at least one explainability technique — Grad-CAM
  recommended (other options: intermediate feature-map visualization, saliency maps, integrated
  gradients, occlusion analysis; a non-Grad-CAM choice must be justified).
  Note: When designing custom architectures using Keras Subclassing (§3.1 & 3.4), ensure that
  intermediate layer outputs — specifically the final convolutional feature map — remain accessible
  via tf.GradientTape() or an intermediate sub-model. Fully encapsulated models that hide convolutional
  outputs behind nested blocks will fail to generate Grad-CAM heatmaps.
  Minimum examples per model:
       1 correctly classified infected cell
       1 correctly classified uninfected cell
       1 incorrectly classified image, where available
  Interpret, don't just display, the heatmaps. Address: which regions drive the prediction; whether the
  model focuses on the cell or on background; whether highlighted regions look medically meaningful;
  how explanations differ between the custom ResNet and the pretrained models; what happens to the
  explanation on an incorrect prediction; and whether the results reveal shortcuts or artifacts the model
  may have learned. A heatmap is evidence to question model behavior, not proof of correct reasoning.
